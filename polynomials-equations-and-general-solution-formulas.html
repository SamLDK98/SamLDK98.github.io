Equations and General Solution Formulas

What does it mean to solve an equation?

"When we know the value of the unknown!"

But do we know the value here?

\[x=10-3\]

We still need to perform a mental (or physical) computation if we want to know that \(x=7\).

Consider:

\[\begin{aligned} \text{E1:}\quad & x=7\\ \text{E2:}\quad & x=10-3\\ \text{E3:}\quad & 8x-43=4+9 \end{aligned}\]

All of these equations contain the same information about \(x\).

We might think that an equation is solved when all arithmetic has been performed.

But we would consider \(\text{E2}\) to be solved already, even though a subtraction is still waiting to be done.

\(\text{E3}\) looks more complicated, but really there is just more arithmetic waiting to be done: we need to add \(4\), \(9\) and \(43\) together and divide the sum by \(8\).

So why is \(\text{E2}\) solved but not \(\text{E3}\) if they are both just waiting for some sequence of computations to reveal \(x\)?

Because an equation is solved when the value of the unknown is expressed in terms of known quantities.

It is not necessarily about reducing the amount of computation required to evaluate \(x\), but rearranging the equation into a particular form.

This is why solving is synonymous with isolating the unknown (as long as no unknown is left on the other side). (\(x=3-x\) has \(x\) on its own on the left, but it isn't solved: the right-hand side still depends on \(x\).)

What is a general solutions formula?

Algebra allows us to abstract structure from specific cases to express general forms.

Consider these equations:

\[\begin{aligned} 2x+5&=25\\ 3x+7&=19\\ 5x-8&=12 \end{aligned}\]

They contain different numbers, but share the same structure.

(Subtraction fits too: \(5x-8\) is \(5x+(-8)\). Subtracting is adding a negative.)

We can express the shared structure as an algebraic equation:

\[ax+b=c\]

Here \(x\) is still the unknown, while \(a\), \(b\) and \(c\) stand for known numbers.

A general solution formula takes the shared structure and rearranges it so that the unknown is expressed in terms of the known quantities:

\[x=\frac{c-b}{a}\qquad (a\neq 0)\]

Notice that a general solution formula is just an equation solved algebraically: \(x\) has been expressed in terms of the known quantities \(a\), \(b\) and \(c\).

One formula now solves all three equations at once:

\[x=\frac{25-5}{2}=10\qquad x=\frac{19-7}{3}=4\qquad x=\frac{12-(-8)}{5}=4\]

Instead of solving equations case by case, we can solve every case of a particular structure.
