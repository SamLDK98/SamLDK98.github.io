Exponentials

Walking in Additive and Multiplicative Steps

Additive Steps

\[256\]

Let's say we want to take steps of equal distance to reach this number.

We start at \(0\).

  • If our steps are a distance of \(1\), it will take \(256\) steps.
  • If our steps are a distance of \(2\), it will take \(128\) steps.
  • If our steps are a distance of \(4\), it will take \(64\) steps.

Division

Division finds the number of steps of equal distance required to reach a number, given the distance of each step.

\[\frac{256}{2}\]

If we move in steps of distance \(2\), how many must be taken to reach \(256\)?

Multiplicative Steps

\[256\]

Let's say we want to take steps of equal ratios to reach this number.

Each step must always be \(x\) times as big as the previous number.

We start at \(1\).

If our steps must have a ratio of \(2\), it will take \(8\) steps.

\[1 \to 2 \to 4 \to 8 \to 16 \to 32 \to 64 \to 128 \to 256\]

If our steps must have a ratio of \(4\), it will take \(4\) steps.

\[1 \to 4 \to 16 \to 64 \to 256\]

If our steps must have a ratio of \(16\), it will take \(2\) steps.

\[1 \to 16 \to 256\]

Logarithms

A logarithm finds the number of steps of equal ratios required to reach a number, given the ratio.

\[\log_{\class{mjx-base}{4}}(256)\]

If we move in steps with a ratio of \(\class{mjx-base}{4}\) between them, how many must be taken to reach \(256\)?

Radication

Taking the root finds the common ratio needed to reach a number, given the number of steps.

\[\sqrt[\class{mjx-exp}{3}]{27}=\ ?\]

Here we know that we want to take \(\class{mjx-exp}{3}\) multiplicative steps and our target value is \(27\), so what must our common ratio be?
