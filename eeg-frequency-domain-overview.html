An Overview of Frequency Domain Analyses

What oscillations is this signal made of?

Two Descriptions of One Signal

An EEG electrode records a voltage that changes over time.

Plotted against time, this is the time domain: the voltage at each moment.

The same signal can also be written as a sum of oscillations, each with its own frequency.

\[x(t) = \class{mjx-amp}{A_1}\sin(2\pi \class{mjx-freq}{f_1} t + \phi_1) + \class{mjx-amp}{A_2}\sin(2\pi \class{mjx-freq}{f_2} t + \phi_2) + \cdots\]

Listing how much of each frequency the signal contains gives the frequency domain.

Neither description holds more information than the other. They are two ways of writing the same signal.

In the widget above, the wiggles in the top plot and the stems in the bottom plot are the same three oscillations.

The step from one description to the other is the Fourier transform.

Any recording of finite length, however irregular, can be written as a sum of oscillations in this way.

Describing an Oscillation

Each oscillation in the sum is described by three numbers:

  • Frequency: how many cycles it completes per second, in hertz (Hz).
  • Amplitude: how far it swings from zero, in microvolts (µV).
  • Phase: where in its cycle it is at a given moment.

Frequency domain analyses ask questions about one or more of these.

Frequency Bands

EEG researchers group frequencies into named bands.

BandRangeOften associated with
Delta\(1\)–\(4\) HzDeep sleep
Theta\(4\)–\(8\) HzDrowsiness, memory tasks
Alpha\(8\)–\(13\) HzRelaxed wakefulness, eyes closed
Beta\(13\)–\(30\) HzActive thinking, movement
Gammaabove \(30\) HzSensory processing

The boundaries are conventions, and they vary a little between studies.

Many effects are easier to see as a change in one band than as a change in the raw trace.

  • Closing the eyes increases alpha over the back of the head.
  • Benzodiazepines, which enhance GABAA receptor activity, reliably increase beta.

The Family of Analyses

Each analysis in this chapter asks a different question of the same oscillations.

AnalysisThe question it asks
Power spectral densityHow is the signal’s power spread across frequencies?
Time–frequency analysisHow does that spread change from moment to moment?
Coherence and connectivityDo two channels oscillate together at a given frequency?
Cross-frequency couplingDoes the phase of a slow rhythm shape the amplitude of a fast one?
The aperiodic componentWhat does the background look like once the peaks are set aside?

The first, and the most widely used, is the power spectral density.

It sets aside phase entirely and asks only how much of each frequency the signal contains.
