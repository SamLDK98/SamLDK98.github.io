Exponentials

Zero and Negative Exponents

The Zero Exponent

What should a number raised to the zeroth power equal?

PowerValue
\(4^5\)\(1{,}024\)
\(4^4\)\(256\)
\(4^3\)\(64\)
\(4^2\)\(16\)
\(4^1\)\(4\)
\(4^0\)?

Notice that:

  • Increasing the exponent multiplies the previous power's value by \(4\).
  • Decreasing the exponent divides the previous power's value by \(4\).

The next entry must be: \(\frac{4}{4} = 1\)

\[4^0 = 1\]

Raising any nonzero base to the power of zero equals \(1\).

\[\boxed{a^0=1 \qquad (a \ne 0)}\]

Negative Integer Exponents

If we continue dividing the preceding value by the base, negative exponents become the reciprocal of the base raised to the positive power.

PowerValue
\(4^{5}\)\(1{,}024\)
\(4^{4}\)\(256\)
\(4^{3}\)\(64\)
\(4^{2}\)\(16\)
\(4^{1}\)\(4\)
\(4^{0}\)\(1\)
\(4^{-1}\)\(\frac{1}{4}\)
\(4^{-2}\)\(\frac{1}{16}\)
\(4^{-3}\)\(\frac{1}{64}\)
\(4^{-4}\)\(\frac{1}{256}\)
\(4^{-5}\)\(\frac{1}{1{,}024}\)

The general identity:

\[\boxed{a^{-n} = \frac{1}{a^n} \qquad (a \ne 0)}\]

Expanded example:

\[a^{-3} = \frac{1}{a} \times \frac{1}{a} \times \frac{1}{a} = \frac{1}{a^3}\]
