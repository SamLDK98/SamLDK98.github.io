Exponentials

Our overarching goal is to understand Euler's identity:

\(e^{i\pi} + 1 = 0\)

First, we need to understand what it means to raise a number to different types of exponents:

\(2^{3}\) \(2^{0}\) \(2^{-3}\) \(2^{\frac{1}{2}}\) \(2^{\sqrt{2}}\) \(2^{i}\)

We'll also explore what makes the constant \(e\) significant, and how exponentiation connects to circular motion.

Two concepts will be particularly important throughout: multiplicative growth and fractional exponents. Developing a clear understanding of each will be key to understanding this section.
