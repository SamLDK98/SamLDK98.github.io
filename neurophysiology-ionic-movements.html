How Ionic Movements Produce Electrical Signals

Before a neuron can spike, it has to be a battery. This is where the voltage across the membrane comes from in the first place — and why moving a vanishingly small number of ions is enough to move it.

The questionWhere does the voltage come from?

A neuron at rest sits at about −65 mV. Chapters 01 and 03 both take that number as a given and build on top of it. This page goes underneath it.

Two facts do all the work. The cell keeps potassium concentrated inside and sodium concentrated outside, and the membrane is far more permeable to some ions than others. Put a concentration gradient across a selectively permeable barrier and you get a voltage — not as a metaphor, but as a measurable consequence of charges separating.
