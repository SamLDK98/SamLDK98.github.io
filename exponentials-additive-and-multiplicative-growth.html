Exponentials

Additive and Multiplicative Growth

In additive growth, each step adds the same amount. The distance between each step is equal.

\[1 \to 3 \to 5 \to 7 \to 9\]

In multiplicative growth, each step multiplies by the same factor.

The distance between each step is not equal.

However, the ratio between steps is always constant.

\[1 \to 2 \to 4 \to 8 \to 16\]

Let's visualise growing by a constant factor of \(\class{mjx-base}{4}\).

Imagine a square.

A grid with four cells appears and the square occupies one of its cells.

A copy of the square then fills each empty cell.

We have grown from \(1 \to 4\).

The grid then vanishes while the square of area \(4\) remains.

The grid then appears again. Our new, bigger square occupies one of its cells.

The grid then fills in again with three more copies of the bigger square.

The square has grown from \(4 \to 16\).

Although the absolute increase from one square to the next varies, the next square is always \(\class{mjx-base}{4}\) times as big as the previous one.

The grid could have any number of cells to represent any multiplicative factor.

Progress in Additive Journeys

Say we start at:

\[0\]

We want to reach:

\[256\]

We must take steps to get there.

As we progress, we will increase our steps taken and our current output.

If our steps are of equal distance, the percentage of steps we have taken will correspond exactly to the percentage of the output we have reached.

For example:

  • If each step travels \(+4\), it will take \(\class{mjx-exp}{64}\) steps to reach \(256\).
  • If we have taken \(25\%\) of our steps, our output will be \(25\%\) of the target.
\[\frac{\class{mjx-exp}{16}}{\class{mjx-exp}{64}} \text{ steps} \to 64\]

Since our steps are of an equal distance, the value of the output when we have taken \(50\%\) of our steps will be \(50\%\) of the value of the target output.

So adding the value of the output after \(50\%\) of our steps to itself will give \(100\%\) of the target output.

\[128 + 128 = 256\]

Progress in Multiplicative Journeys

Say we start at:

\[1\]

We want to reach:

\[256\]

We must take steps to get there.

As we progress, we will increase our steps taken and our current output.

If our steps are of an equal ratio, the percentage of steps we have taken will not correspond to the percentage of the output we have reached (at least not directly).

If each step multiplies our previous output by a factor of \(\class{mjx-base}{2}\), it will take \(\class{mjx-exp}{8}\) steps.

\[1 \to 2 \to 4 \to 8 \to 16 \to 32 \to 64 \to 128 \to 256\]

If we take \(25\%\) of our steps (\(\class{mjx-exp}{2}\) steps), our output will be \(4\), while \(25\%\) of the target output is \(64\).

When we have taken \(50\%\) of our steps, our output is still only \(16\)!

In fact, we will not have reached the halfway point of our output (\(128\)) until we have taken \(87.5\%\) of our multiplicative journey's steps.

However, although we are not halfway to our output in absolute distance, we are halfway in another sense.

In an additive journey, halfway through the steps means halfway to the output in terms of distance.

In a multiplicative journey, halfway through the steps means halfway to the output in terms of ratio.

The ratio from the beginning of the journey to the halfway point will be the same as the ratio from the halfway point to the end.

\[\frac{16}{1} = \frac{256}{16} = 16\]

That means multiplying the output at the halfway point by itself will reach the target output.

\[16 \times 16 = 256\]

We call the output at the halfway point of multiplicative steps the geometric mean.

Main Takeaway

We can think of journeys between values as comprised of steps.

These steps can be additive or multiplicative.

Additive steps have a direct correspondence with the distance from the target output.

If we add the output that we reach when we have taken half our additive steps to itself, we will reach the target output.

Multiplicative steps still have a correspondence with the distance from the target output, although it does not match one-to-one.

If we multiply the output that we reach when we have taken half our multiplicative steps by itself, we will reach the target output.
