Exponentials

Additive and Multiplicative Growth

We can think of journeys between values as comprised of steps.

These steps can be additive or multiplicative.

In additive growth, each step adds the same amount. The distance between each step is equal.

In multiplicative growth, each step multiplies by the same factor.

The distance between each step is not equal.

However, the ratio between steps is always constant.

Let's visualise growing by a constant factor of \(\class{mjx-base}{4}\).

The grid represents the factor we are increasing by. It could have any number of cells to represent any factor, but here it was four. We can see that although the absolute increase from one square to the next varies, the next square is always \(4\) times as big as the previous one.

Progress in Additive Journeys

Start: \(0\)
Target: \(256\)
Step size: \(+4\)

If we move in steps of equal distance, the percentage of steps taken is always the same as the percentage of the output reached.

The value of the output when we are halfway through our steps will be \(50\%\) of the target output. We could add this value to itself to reach the target output.

Progress in Multiplicative Journeys

Start: \(1\)
Target: \(256\)
Step size: \(\times\class{mjx-base}{2}\)

If we move in steps of equal ratio, the percentage of steps taken does not match the percentage of the output reached. Halfway through our steps, the output is still only \(16\), and we don't reach half the target (\(128\)) until we have taken \(87.5\%\) of our steps.

But halfway through the steps is halfway in another sense: in terms of ratio. From \(1\) to \(16\) is \(\times 16\), and from \(16\) to \(256\) is \(\times 16\) again. We could multiply the value at the halfway point by itself to reach the target output.

We call the output at the halfway point of multiplicative steps the geometric mean.
