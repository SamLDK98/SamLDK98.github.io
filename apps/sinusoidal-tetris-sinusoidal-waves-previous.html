Sinusoidal Waves

Imagine a point travelling around a circle at a constant speed.

If we plot its height on the y-axis and time on the x-axis, we will graph a sinusoidal wave.

Three parameters may vary:

  • The speed the point travels around the circle.
  • The size of the circle.
  • The place the point begins travelling from.

The speed at which the point travels around the circle is described by its angular frequency \((\omega)\). Increasing this speed increases the frequency \((f)\) of the wave: the number of complete oscillations (or cycles) that occur per second.

The radius of the circle determines the amplitude \((A)\) of the wave: the maximum height of its peaks above the centre line (or the maximum depth of its troughs below it).

The position from which the point begins travelling determines the initial phase \((\phi)\) of the wave. Changing it shifts the wave horizontally, altering where in its cycle the wave begins.
