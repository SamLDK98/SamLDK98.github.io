Sinusoidal Tetris

Flattening a Signal

In Sinusoidal Tetris, a wiggly signal sits at the bottom of the board.

Sine waves fall from the top, one at a time. Each one lands on the signal and is added to it.

The aim is to choose each falling wave so that the signal ends up flat.

Three Dials

Every falling wave is a sine wave with three dials you can turn:

\[y(t) = A \sin(\omega t + \varphi)\]
  • \(A\), the amplitude: how tall the wave is.
  • \(\omega\), the frequency: how quickly it wiggles.
  • \(\varphi\), the phase: how far along its cycle it starts.

Flattening a Signal, Step by Step

Here is the easiest possible game: a signal made of just two sine waves.

The Idea

Each wave is cancelled by its mirror image: the same amplitude and the same frequency, with the phase moved half a turn.

\[A \sin(\omega t + \varphi) + A \sin(\omega t + \varphi + \pi) = 0\]

Half a turn flips the wave upside down, so wherever one is up, the other is down by the same amount.

A complicated signal is just several waves added together, so it can be flattened one wave at a time.

Get any dial slightly wrong and a small wave is left behind. In the game, that leftover becomes part of the signal you have to deal with next.
