Exponentials

Exponentiation Notation

Defining exponentiation succinctly in plain language is surprisingly tricky.

It is often introduced as repeated multiplication where the exponent denotes a number of copies of the base to include in a product.

\[x^2 \rightarrow x^3\]
\[8 \cdot 8 \rightarrow 8 \cdot 8 \cdot 8\]

This works for positive integer exponents, but becomes confusing when we try to apply it to fractional exponents.

A cleaner way to conceptualise exponentiation is to think in terms of multiplicative growth journeys.

We can think of powers as starting at \(1\) on our number line. The above would read, “start at \(1\) and take \(4\) steps at a step ratio of \(3\)”.

Introducing Exponent Notation to a Multiplicative Journey

  • Start: \(1\).
  • Step ratio: \(\times 4\).
  • Number of steps: \(5\).

Outputs:

\[1 \rightarrow 4 \rightarrow 16 \rightarrow 64 \rightarrow 256 \rightarrow 1{,}024\]

Steps:

\[0 \rightarrow 1 \rightarrow 2 \rightarrow 3 \rightarrow 4 \rightarrow 5\]

We can replace our step labels with exponents, using \(4\) as the base. These powers correspond to the above outputs.

\[4^0 \rightarrow 4^1 \rightarrow 4^2 \rightarrow 4^3 \rightarrow 4^4 \rightarrow 4^5\]
