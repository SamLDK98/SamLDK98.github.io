Exponentials

The Laws of Exponents

The following laws allow us to simplify our calculations involving multiplication, division or raising powers to other powers.

These laws are not arbitrary. They follow from the properties of multiplication, as we can demonstrate by expanding and rearranging the factors.

Product of Powers Rule

\[\boxed{a^m \times a^n = a^{m+n}}\]

When multiplying powers with identical bases, we combine their factors into a single product.

\[5^2\cdot5^3=(5\cdot5)(5\cdot5\cdot5)=5^5\]

The resulting exponent is the sum of the original exponents.

\[5^{2+3}=5^5\]

Quotient of Powers Rule

\[\boxed{\frac{a^m}{a^n}=a^{m-n}\qquad(a\ne0)}\]

When dividing powers with identical bases, matching factors in the numerator and denominator cancel.

The number of factors remaining is determined by subtracting the denominator’s exponent from the numerator’s exponent.

For example:

\[\frac{3^5}{3^2}=3^{5-2}=3^3\]

Expanding the powers shows why:

\[\frac{3\times3\times3\times3\times3}{3\times3}\]

Two factors in the numerator cancel with two in the denominator, leaving three factors of 3.

\[=3\times3\times3=3^3\]

Power of a Power Rule

\[\boxed{(a^m)^n=a^{mn}}\]

Raising a power to another power means repeating the entire original product. The total number of factors is therefore the product of the two exponents.

For example:

\[(2^3)^2\]

We know that \(2^3\) represents three copies of 2 multiplied together:

\[2^3=2\times2\times2\]

Raising this entire expression to the power of 2 means taking two copies of that product:

\[(2\times2\times2)\times(2\times2\times2)\]

We now have two groups of three factors, giving six factors altogether:

\[(2^3)^2=2^{3\times2}=2^6\]

Power of a Product Rule

\[\boxed{(ab)^n=a^nb^n}\]

Raising a product to a power is equivalent to raising each factor to that power and then multiplying the results together.

For example:

\[(2\cdot3)^3\]

This means taking three copies of the entire product:

\[(2\cdot3)(2\cdot3)(2\cdot3)\]

We can rearrange the factors to group identical numbers together:

\[(2\cdot2\cdot2)(3\cdot3\cdot3)\]

Therefore:

\[(2\cdot3)^3=2^3\cdot3^3\]

Power of a Quotient Rule

\[\boxed{\left(\frac{a}{b}\right)^n=\frac{a^n}{b^n}\qquad(b\ne0)}\]

Raising a quotient to a power is equivalent to raising the numerator and denominator separately to that power.

For example:

\[\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^3\]

This means multiplying three copies of the fraction together:

\[\frac{2}{3}\cdot\frac{2}{3}\cdot\frac{2}{3}\]

When multiplying fractions, we multiply their numerators together and their denominators together:

\[\frac{2\cdot2\cdot2}{3\cdot3\cdot3}\]

Therefore:

\[\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^3=\frac{2^3}{3^3}=\frac{8}{27}\]
