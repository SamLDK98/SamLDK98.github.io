Exponentials

Laws of Exponents

As the exponent increases additively, the corresponding value of the power increases multiplicatively.

The step size for the exponent's journey is just \(1\).

The step ratio depends on the base.

\[2^0 = 1, \quad 2^1 = 2, \quad 2^2 = 4, \quad 2^3 = 8\]

Product of Powers Rule

Recall that steps combine through multiplication and apply the same factor to the output regardless of position on the number line.

\[5^2 = 25, \quad 5^3 = 125\]

These powers represent combined factors:

\[\underbrace{1 \cdot 5 \cdot 5}_{2\text{ steps}} \qquad \underbrace{1 \cdot 5 \cdot 5 \cdot 5}_{3\text{ steps}}\]

Multiplying them together is equivalent to combining the two journeys:

\[5^2 \cdot 5^3 = 5^{2+3} = 5^5\]

The exponents add because we're combining the number of steps, while the outputs multiply because we're combining the multiplicative factors.

\[\boxed{a^m \times a^n = a^{m+n}}\]

When multiplying powers with identical bases, we combine their factors into a single product.

Quotient of Powers Rule

Recall that the ratio between two outputs depends only on the number of steps between them, regardless of where they sit on the number line.

\[7^4 = 2401, \quad 7^2 = 49\]

Dividing one power by the other compares the output after \(4\) steps with the output after \(2\) steps. Two steps lie between them:

\[\frac{7^4}{7^2} = 7^{4-2} = 7^2\]
\[\boxed{\frac{a^m}{a^n}=a^{m-n}\qquad(a\ne0)}\]

When dividing powers with identical bases, matching factors in the numerator and denominator cancel.

This corresponds with subtracting the bottom exponent from the top.

Power of a Power Rule

Recall that a single multiplicative step can be made up of several smaller ones.

Each step of \(\times 2^3\) is itself \(3\) steps of \(\times 2\). Taking \(2\) of these larger steps takes \(3 \times 2\) steps of \(\times 2\):

\[\bigl(2^3\bigr)^2 = 2^{3 \times 2} = 2^6\]
\[\boxed{(a^m)^n=a^{mn}}\]

Raising a power to another power means repeating the entire original product. The total number of factors is therefore the product of the two exponents.

The same grouping lets one output be reached by many journeys. One step of \(\times 16\) is two steps of \(\times 4\), or four steps of \(\times 2\):

\[256 = 256^1 = 16^2 = 4^4 = 2^8\]

Power of a Product Rule

\[\boxed{(ab)^n=a^nb^n}\]

Raising a product to a power is equivalent to raising each factor to that power and then multiplying the results together.

Power of a Quotient Rule

\[\boxed{\left(\frac{a}{b}\right)^n=\frac{a^n}{b^n}\qquad(b\ne0)}\]

Raising a quotient to a power is equivalent to raising the numerator and denominator separately to that power.

For example:

\[\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^3\]

This means multiplying three copies of the fraction together:

\[\frac{2}{3}\cdot\frac{2}{3}\cdot\frac{2}{3}\]

When multiplying fractions, we multiply their numerators together and their denominators together:

\[\frac{2\cdot2\cdot2}{3\cdot3\cdot3}\]

Therefore:

\[\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^3=\frac{2^3}{3^3}=\frac{8}{27}\]
