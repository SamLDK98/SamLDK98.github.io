Exponentials

The Many Rules of Exponentiation

Product of Powers Rule

\[\boxed{a^{\class{mjx-exp}{m}} \times a^{\class{mjx-exp}{n}} = a^{\class{mjx-exp}{m + n}}}\]

Multiplying powers with identical bases

\[5^2 \cdot 5^3\]

Gives the same value as raising the base to the sum of their exponents

\[5^{2+3}=5^5\]

Quotient Rule

Similarly, dividing powers with the same base behaves the same as subtracting their exponents.

This is captured by the quotient of powers rule (for a nonzero base \(a\)):

\[\frac{a^{\class{mjx-exp}{m}}}{a^{\class{mjx-exp}{n}}} = a^{\class{mjx-exp}{m-n}} \qquad (a \ne 0)\]

Power of a Power Rule

\[\left(a^{\class{mjx-exp}{m}}\right)^{\class{mjx-exp}{n}}=a^{\class{mjx-exp}{mn}}\]

Power of a Product Rule

\[(ab)^{\class{mjx-exp}{n}}= a^{\class{mjx-exp}{n}} b^{\class{mjx-exp}{n}}\]

Power of a Quotient Rule

\[\left( \frac{a}{b} \right)^{\class{mjx-exp}{n}} = \frac{a^{\class{mjx-exp}{n}}}{b^{\class{mjx-exp}{n}}} \qquad (b \ne 0)\]
