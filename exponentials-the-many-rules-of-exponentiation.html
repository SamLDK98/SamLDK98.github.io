Exponentials

The Many Rules of Exponentiation

Product of Powers Rule

We can observe something interesting if we write up the sequential powers of a number in a table.

PowerValue
\(5^1\)\(5\)
\(5^2\)\(25\)
\(5^3\)\(125\)
\(5^4\)\(625\)
\(5^5\)\(3{,}125\)
\(5^6\)\(15{,}625\)
\(5^7\)\(78{,}125\)
\(5^8\)\(390{,}625\)
\(5^9\)\(1{,}953{,}125\)
\(5^{10}\)\(9{,}765{,}625\)

Multiplying powers of the same base gives the same value as raising the base number to the sum of their exponents.

\(5^2\times5^3\) is the same thing as \(5^5\):

\[25\cdot125=3{,}125\]

Counting copies shows why: two copies of \(5\) and three more make five.

\[5^2\times5^3=(5\cdot5)\times(5\cdot5\cdot5)=5^5\]

This is captured by the product of powers rule:

\[\boxed{a^{\class{mjx-exp}{m}}\times a^{\class{mjx-exp}{n}}=a^{\class{mjx-exp}{m+n}}}\]

Notice the base must be identical!

The rule holds for the exponents of the earlier pages too. \(5^{\frac{1}{2}}\times5^{\frac{1}{2}}=5^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2}}=5^1\) is the equation that gave \(5^{\frac{1}{2}}=\sqrt{5}\) in The Root Rule.

Quotient of Powers Rule

Similarly, dividing powers with the same base behaves the same as subtracting their exponents.

Dividing cancels copies:

\[\frac{5^5}{5^2}=\frac{\cancel{5}\cdot\cancel{5}\cdot5\cdot5\cdot5}{\cancel{5}\cdot\cancel{5}}=5^3\]

This is captured by the quotient of powers rule (for a nonzero base \(a\)):

\[\boxed{\frac{a^{\class{mjx-exp}{m}}}{a^{\class{mjx-exp}{n}}}=a^{\class{mjx-exp}{m-n}}}\]

When \(m=n\), it gives \(\frac{a^n}{a^n}=a^0=1\). When \(m<n\), it gives a negative exponent: \(\frac{5^2}{5^5}=5^{-3}=\frac{1}{5^3}\). These are the meanings from the first page: they are the ones that keep this rule true.

Power of a Power Rule

\((5^2)^3\) is three copies of \(5^2\), each holding two copies of \(5\):

\[\begin{aligned}(5^2)^3&=5^2\cdot5^2\cdot5^2\\&=(5\cdot5)(5\cdot5)(5\cdot5)=5^6\end{aligned}\]
\[\boxed{\left(a^{\class{mjx-exp}{m}}\right)^{\class{mjx-exp}{n}}=a^{\class{mjx-exp}{mn}}}\]

This also gives the two ways of writing a fractional exponent:

\[\begin{aligned}a^{\frac{m}{n}}&=\left(a^{\frac{1}{n}}\right)^m=\left(\sqrt[n]{a}\right)^m\\a^{\frac{m}{n}}&=\left(a^m\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}=\sqrt[n]{a^m}\end{aligned}\]

Power of a Product Rule

Three copies of \(ab\) hold three copies of \(a\) and three of \(b\):

\[\begin{aligned}(ab)^3&=ab\cdot ab\cdot ab\\&=a\cdot a\cdot a\cdot b\cdot b\cdot b=a^3b^3\end{aligned}\]
\[\boxed{(ab)^{\class{mjx-exp}{n}}=a^{\class{mjx-exp}{n}}b^{\class{mjx-exp}{n}}}\]

Power of a Quotient Rule

Likewise for a quotient:

\[\left(\frac{a}{b}\right)^3=\frac{a}{b}\cdot\frac{a}{b}\cdot\frac{a}{b}=\frac{a\cdot a\cdot a}{b\cdot b\cdot b}=\frac{a^3}{b^3}\]

For \(b\ne0\):

\[\boxed{\left(\frac{a}{b}\right)^{\class{mjx-exp}{n}}=\frac{a^{\class{mjx-exp}{n}}}{b^{\class{mjx-exp}{n}}}}\]
