Exponentials

Laws of Exponents

These laws follow from the properties of multiplication, as we will demonstrate by expanding and rearranging the factors below.

Product of Powers Rule

\[\boxed{a^m \times a^n = a^{m+n}}\]

When multiplying powers with identical bases, we combine their factors into a single product.

\[5^2 \cdot 5^3\]
\[(5 \cdot 5)(5 \cdot 5 \cdot 5)\]
\[5^5\]

The resulting exponent is the sum of the original exponents.

\[5^{2+3}=5^5\]

Quotient of Powers Rule

\[\boxed{\frac{a^m}{a^n}=a^{m-n}\qquad(a\ne0)}\]

When dividing powers with identical bases, matching factors in the numerator and denominator cancel.

This corresponds with subtracting the bottom exponent from the top.

Power of a Power Rule

\[\boxed{(a^m)^n=a^{mn}}\]

Raising a power to another power means repeating the entire original product. The total number of factors is therefore the product of the two exponents.

Power of a Product Rule

\[\boxed{(ab)^n=a^nb^n}\]

Raising a product to a power is equivalent to raising each factor to that power and then multiplying the results together.

Power of a Quotient Rule

\[\boxed{\left(\frac{a}{b}\right)^n=\frac{a^n}{b^n}\qquad(b\ne0)}\]

Raising a quotient to a power is equivalent to raising the numerator and denominator separately to that power.

For example:

\[\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^3\]

This means multiplying three copies of the fraction together:

\[\frac{2}{3}\cdot\frac{2}{3}\cdot\frac{2}{3}\]

When multiplying fractions, we multiply their numerators together and their denominators together:

\[\frac{2\cdot2\cdot2}{3\cdot3\cdot3}\]

Therefore:

\[\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^3=\frac{2^3}{3^3}=\frac{8}{27}\]
