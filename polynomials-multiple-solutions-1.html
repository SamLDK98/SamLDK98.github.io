Polynomials

Multiple Solutions (1)

Undoing Gives One Answer

Linear equations have exactly one solution (as long as \(a\neq0\)).

\[2x+4=7\]

Why only one?

Because we can express \(x\) in terms of known quantities. The left-hand side is built from \(x\) in two steps: multiply by \(2\), then add \(4\). To find \(x\), we undo the steps in reverse order:

\[x=\frac{7-4}{2}=1.5\]

Every step can be undone perfectly. Adding a number is undone by subtracting it. Multiplying by a non-zero number is undone by dividing by it.

These operations are bijective: they pair inputs with outputs one-to-one, so different inputs always give different outputs.

If adding \(4\) to a number gives \(7\), there is only one number it could have been: \(3\).

So each time we undo a step, there is only one number to go back to. The chain ends at a single value of \(x\).

Squaring Is Many-to-One

Once \(x^2\) appears, one of the steps can't be undone this way.

Squaring is many-to-one: different inputs can give the same output.

\[2\cdot2=4\qquad(-2)\cdot(-2)=4\]

So knowing the output does not tell us the input. If \(x\cdot x=4\), then \(x=2\) or \(x=-2\).

Every positive number has two square roots, one positive and one negative. Undoing a square gives two candidates, not one. This is where a second solution comes from.

The two solutions of \(x^2=4\) are opposites. They sit the same distance either side of \(0\), because squaring ignores sign: \(x\) and \(-x\) always give the same output.

The Mirror Moves

Now take:

\[x^2-5x=-6\]

Its solutions are \(2\) and \(3\). They are not opposites. So where did the symmetry go?

It moved. \(x^2-5x\) is a square in disguise:

\[x^2-5x=(x-2.5)^2-6.25\]

(Expand the right-hand side to check: \(x^2-5x+6.25-6.25\).)

So the equation is really:

\[\begin{aligned} (x-2.5)^2-6.25&=-6\\ (x-2.5)^2&=0.25 \end{aligned}\]

Undo the square, and there are two possibilities again:

\[\begin{aligned} x-2.5=0.5&\quad\text{or}\quad x-2.5=-0.5\\ x=3&\quad\text{or}\quad x=2 \end{aligned}\]

The two solutions are mirror images, just as before. But the mirror is at \(2.5\), not \(0\). Both \(2\) and \(3\) are \(0.5\) away from it.

The \(-5x\) term does not create the second solution. Squaring does. The \(-5x\) term moves the mirror.

In \(2x+4\), each unit increase in \(x\) changes the output by the same amount, so no output repeats. In \(x^2-5x\), the change is different each time: the outputs fall until \(2.5\), then rise, retracing the same values on the other side of the mirror. (Multiple Solutions follows this rise and fall step by step.)

If the right-hand side is exactly the lowest output, \(-6.25\), the two mirror images meet at \(2.5\) and there is one solution. Below that, there are none.

The Last Step Is an “Or”

A linear equation can be solved with arithmetic alone: \(x=\frac{7-4}{2}\).

A quadratic can't be undone step by step in the same way. Move everything to one side, and \(x^2-5x=-6\) becomes \(x^2-5x+6=0\). The unknown sits in two unlike terms, \(x^2\) and \(-5x\), so there is no single chain of operations to reverse.

There are two ways around this, and both end with a logical step rather than a calculation.

Factoring. \(x^2-5x+6=0\) becomes \((x-2)(x-3)=0\). The zero-product law says that if a product of two numbers is zero, at least one of the factors is zero. So \(x-2=0\) or \(x-3=0\), which gives \(x=2\) or \(x=3\).

Completing the square. Rewrite the equation so that \(x\) appears only once, as above: \((x-2.5)^2=0.25\). Now there is a single chain to reverse, but one of its steps is undoing a square, and that step gives two possibilities.

Either way, the answer is not a single number calculated from the knowns. It is a choice: \(x=2\) or \(x=3\).
